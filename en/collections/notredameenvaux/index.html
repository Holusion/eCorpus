Collection of sculptures from Notre-Dame-en-Vaux in Châlons-en-Champagne

About

The cloister of the collegiate church of Notre-Dame-en-Vaux once housed a unique collection of finely carved column-statues depicting scenes from the Old and New Testaments. Built around 1170–1180, they served the canons as objects of contemplation and education, although this fashion, tied to early Gothic art, was short-lived and quickly abandoned during subsequent liturgical reforms.

Severely lacking funds to maintain their cloister in the 18th century, the monks carried out its destruction between 1759 and 1766. The cloister was torn down and the land rebuilt, using the debris for foundations. The cloister and its sculptures fell into oblivion for two centuries and escaped the destruction of the Revolutionary period. Rediscovered in fragments during excavations led by Léon Pressouyre from 1963 to 1976, the statues were reassembled and completed with metaline.
