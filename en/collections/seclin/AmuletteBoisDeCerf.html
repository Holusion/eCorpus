Description

This red deer antler amulet-pendant is truncated-conical with a circular base. Its decoration consists of two series of four grooves framing a band of lozenges or crosses.

It was found in the fill of an anthropomorphic burial in the parish cemetery of the 10th-11th centuries, but may be older, dating between the 4th and 7th centuries. This type of apotropaic amulet is generally found in female burials, worn as a pendant or an earring, and still bears witness to pagan rites.
