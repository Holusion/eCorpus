## Advanced guide to 3D Materials

A 3D object is made of digital data intended to recreate a surface in relief. This surface is rendered by a mesh composed of polygons, in particular triangles and <span style="text-decoration: underline dotted; cursor: help;" title="A quad is a polygonal structure made of 4 sides, that is two triangles">**quads**</span>.

<img src="/assets/img/doc/Blender_Mesh_01.jpg" width ="35%" alt="diagram of the composition of a 3D polygon"/>

A mesh is therefore made of faces, <span style="text-decoration: underline dotted; cursor: help;" title="An edge corresponds to the ridge of a shape">**edges**</span> and <span style="text-decoration: underline dotted; cursor: help;" title="A vertex corresponds to the corner of a shape">**vertices**</span>, and can be more or less complex depending on the number of polygons it is made of.

*For technical reasons, only quads and triangles are used when creating a 3D mesh. Any polygon with more than 4 vertices, also called an N-Gon, must be avoided.*


### What is a 3D mesh topology?

The **topology** of a 3D surface refers to the **organisation of edges and vertices** within a mesh. A bad topology can cause errors as well as various problems when editing your 3D model (particularly if the model contains <span style="text-decoration: underline dotted; cursor: help;" title="A polygon made of more than 4 vertices">**N-Gons**</span> or <span style="text-decoration: underline dotted; cursor: help;" title="Typically, a vertex or an edge that does not seem to be connected to any other point of the structure">**Loose**</span> points).

Conversely, a good topology allows for faster and cleaner work when editing and <span style="text-decoration: underline dotted; cursor: help;" title="A deformation occurs when the object is animated">**deforming**</span> a 3D mesh.

#### What makes a good topology
A topology considered good is mostly composed of quads (which greatly facilitates so-called "manual" modelling work, unlike triangles) and contains **Loops**.

A loop is a set of faces and edges which, once selected, forms a continuous loop. These loops allow a segment of the 3D model to be selected quickly, and prove very useful for models containing many polygons.

A model containing good loops will deform better once animated, and will be simpler to handle when editing certain parameters.

#### How to work with the topology of scanned objects
Typically, **meshes generated by digitising objects** from point clouds are considered **bad topologies**.

Indeed, the topology is generated according to algorithms built from the information recognised in the point cloud. It can also turn out to be <span style="text-decoration: underline dotted; cursor: help;" title="A Non-Manifold object could not exist in reality, because it contains mathematical information that contradicts the laws of physics">**Non-Manifold**</span>.

* Fortunately, it is still perfectly possible to work with these objects. A Blender editing guide will soon be available to teach you how to easily edit your 3D model according to the constraints brought by this type of object.

* To learn more about the composition of a 3D structure, we invite you to discover the [Blender documentation](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/meshes/structure.html), which is very complete on this subject.
